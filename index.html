Air Traffic Management

Keeping the skies moving, one decision at a time.

Air traffic management sits at the intersection of safety, technology, and constant disruption. In first quarter of 2020 alone, the industry absorbed a drop of roughly 620 million passengers as COVID-19 spread globally, with airline revenues falling by about 55% that year. More recently, shifting tariffs and volatile oil prices have continued to squeeze margins and reshape traffic patterns. For anyone building a career or a business in this field, continuous learning isn't optional, it's how the system stays resilient.

Market & Industry Trends

The ATM sector is growing steadily even as it works through supply, demand, and labor pressures. Here's a snapshot of where the market stands and where it's headed.

Market Valuation

A market on a steady growth curve

According to Fortune Business Insights, the global air traffic management market was valued at approximately USD 15.23 billion in 2025. Forecasts vary by scope and methodology, with projections ranging from roughly USD 15.94 billion to USD 20.5 billion by 2034, reflecting continued investment in modernization and capacity expansion worldwide.

Trend

Digital & virtual towers

Remote and digital tower technology lets controllers manage airspace using camera feeds and sensor fusion instead of a traditional glass tower, enabling smaller airports to share controller resources across sites.

Trend

AI-assisted flow management

Machine learning models are increasingly used to predict congestion, optimize sequencing, and recommend flow restrictions before delays cascade across the network.

Trend

Supply & demand shifts

Post-pandemic recovery has been uneven across regions, and tariff and fuel-price volatility continue to influence route economics, fleet planning, and traffic volumes.

Trend

Controller & technician shortages

Many air navigation service providers report ongoing shortages of certified controllers and navigation technicians, driving renewed investment in training pipelines and retention.

Major Institutions & Companies

Global Standards

ICAO

The International Civil Aviation Organization sets the global standards and recommended practices (through its Annexes) that national ATM systems are built around.

United States

FAA

The Federal Aviation Administration regulates and operates air traffic control across U.S. airspace and runs the FAA Academy that trains every American controller.

Europe

EUROCONTROL

An intergovernmental organization of dozens of member states working toward a "Single European Sky," coordinating ATM technology and procedures across the continent.

Canada

NAV CANADA

The world's first fully privatized air navigation service provider, a private not-for-profit company managing over 18 million square kilometres of Canadian and oceanic airspace.

United Kingdom

NATS

The UK's leading air navigation service provider, handling traffic over Britain and parts of the North Atlantic.

Industry Body

CANSO

The Civil Air Navigation Services Organisation represents air navigation service providers worldwide, advocating for safe, efficient, cost-effective ATM.

Core ATM Services & ATFM

SKYbrary defines air traffic management around three interlocking pillars, each with its own objectives and operating rhythm.

Pillar 1

Air Traffic Services (ATS)

The operational core of ATM: controllers and flight information officers working directly with pilots to keep aircraft safely separated and moving efficiently.

Pillar 2

Air Traffic Flow Management (ATFM)

Ensures optimal traffic flow when demand is expected to exceed the maximum capacity of the ATC system, organizing and handling traffic safely so that airspace and airport capacity are used as effectively as possible.

Pillar 3

Airspace Management (ASM)

Allocates and structures airspace itself both civil and military, so it can be used flexibly and efficiently by all the traffic that needs it.

ATS Objectives

Prevent in-air collisions

Maintain safe separation between aircraft in flight.

Prevent ground collisions

Keep aircraft and vehicles separated on maneuvering areas.

Maintain orderly flow

Expedite and organize traffic to keep the system moving.

Flight info & SAR

Provide flight information and notify search & rescue when needed.

Good to Know

One unit, multiple services

ATS is delivered by designated Air Traffic Service Units (ATSUs), and a single unit often provides more than one type of service at once, for example, an air traffic control unit may also supply flight information and alerting service alongside standard control duties.

Factors Affecting Service Need

Traffic density

How many aircraft are operating in a given volume of airspace.

Traffic type

The mix of commercial, general aviation, and military operations.

Available equipment

Radar, surveillance, and communication technology on hand.

Weather & geography

Terrain and conditions that shape routing and workload.

ATFM Phases

Months ahead

Strategic planning

Carried out after consulting ATC providers and aircraft operators. Examples include boosting ATC capacity for seasonal peaks (e.g. sending controllers from another facility), rerouting certain traffic flows, and rescheduling flights to balance demand.

1 day ahead

Pre-tactical planning

Fine-tunes the strategic plan for the next day. Details are published for all parties involved, covering smaller-scale rerouting, coordination of off-load routes, and confirming which tactical measures will be used.

Day-of

Tactical operations

Executes the agreed measures and monitors their effect in real time. When demand is set to exceed a sector or aerodrome's capacity, the ATC unit alerts the ATFM unit so restrictions can be applied and affected operators can be informed of expected delays.

Educational Pathways, Salaries & Advice

Several routes lead into ATM careers, each with different timelines, requirements, and progression.

Educational Pathways

Pathway · Canada

NAV CANADA direct-entry training

No prior aviation experience or degree is required, NAV CANADA provides all training and pays a salary from day one. Flight service specialist training takes roughly one year; air traffic controller training takes one to two years, split into initial classroom/simulator training and on-the-job training at a live facility. Training pay starts at $58,606 CAD, rising to $61,337 CAD after one year.

navcanada.ca/careers-in-aviation

Pathway · United States

FAA Collegiate Training Initiative (AT-CTI)

A non-funded partnership between the FAA and roughly 30–36 approved colleges and universities (e.g. Embry-Riddle, Purdue Polytechnic) offering two- or four-year aviation degrees with air traffic curricula. Students pay standard college tuition; graduates can skip the first five weeks of training at the FAA Academy in Oklahoma City, which pays an academy trainee stipend of roughly $20–$25/hr.

faa.gov/AT-CTI

Pathway

Post-secondary degrees

Aviation, aeronautics, or related programs at colleges and universities are useful preparation even outside a formal CTI partnership.

Pathway

Military training

Air traffic control experience gained through military service (e.g. Air Force, Navy), often transferable to civilian ANSP roles.

Salary Progression

CAD $104K–$207K

Tower Controllers

Handle aircraft on the ground and near the airport. NAV CANADA's 2026 qualified salary range is roughly $104,258–$207,450 CAD, plus premiums, scaling with facility complexity and seniority.

CAD $143K–$207K

Enroute (Area) Controllers

Manage traffic across wider airspace sectors between airports. NAV CANADA's 2026 qualified range is roughly $143,513–$207,450 CAD, plus IFR premiums of up to $49,344 CAD/year. In the U.S., the FAA's median controller salary was about $132,250 USD in 2022, with top facilities paying well over $180,000 USD by 2026.

USD $70K–$100K+

Navigation / Electronics Technicians

Maintain and calibrate the radar, radio, and navigation equipment controllers depend on. In the U.S., FAA Airway Transportation Systems Specialist roles (GS-0856) typically pay roughly $70,000–$100,000+ USD depending on locality and experience.

Sources: NAV CANADA, Careers in Aviation & Air Traffic Controller pages (2026); Farmingdale State College, FAA AT-CTI Partnership page, citing 2022 FAA median salary data; SimpleFlying, "A Look at the Salaries of Air Traffic Controllers in 2026."

Getting Started

Advice

Starting a career in ATM

  1. Research entry requirements early; medical, aptitude testing, and citizenship rules vary by country and provider.
  2. Apply to a recognized training pathway (ANSP program, CTI school, or military route).
  3. Build strong spatial reasoning and multitasking habits well before formal training starts.
  4. Stay current on the trends above; digital towers and AI tools are changing what the job looks like.
Advice

Launching a business in ATM

  1. Identify a specific gap for example, training simulators, flow-management software, or equipment maintenance are common entry points.
  2. Understand the regulatory environment; ATM is a heavily certified, safety-critical industry.
  3. Build relationships with ANSPs and airports early, since sales cycles in this sector are long.
  4. Watch macro factors like fuel prices and tariffs ripple directly into ATM budgets.
